# Overview

In this series, we'll go through Sutton and Barto's Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction textbook. It serves as a foundational overview of RL algorithms, but as with many popular fields of tech research, advances have since eclipsed most algorithms described in the book. That being said, while most RL implementations do not directly deal with state-value functions and policy iteration directly (and instead use neural networks to model the state and transition space in a more scalable way), it's still crucial to understand the fundamentals of these algorithms. The latter half of the book will dive into deep reinforcement learning up through AlphaGo, but I will dedicate several of the final posts to more modern techniques such as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which is used by LLMs like GPT-4 in training.

The textbook is avaialbe for free online through a Creative Commons license and can be downloaded [here](https://opentechbook.com/book/reinforcement-learning-2nd-edition). I highly recommend reading along with the book as we go through. I won't redefine all of the terminology and will instead focus on solving some of the more interesting and fundamental exercises while also implementing a number of algorithms seen throughout the book.

Thank you for coming along for the journey, and I hope you are able to build a genuine understanding of these revolutionary algorithms and the techniques they rely on.
